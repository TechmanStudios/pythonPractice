## Three basic lights to make your content shine, literally!

Adding purposefully placed lights while filming your content can add that extra bit of sparkle that draws in viewers. Thethree-point lightingtechnique can easily help you add that wow factor. three-point lighting Put these three lights together to set your content up for success:


### Key light

A key light is the main source of artificial light in your scene. It shines on your subject to add depth and focus. A key light should be placed at a 45° angle to the side of your camera, and 45° higher than your subject's eyeline.


### Fill light

A fill light is second to the key light. Place this light on the opposite side of the key light to fill in the shadows created by the key light. A fill light is dimmer than the key light and doesn't create its own shadows.


### Backlight

A backlight is the dimmest light in the scene. Place this light at an angle behind the subject to create separation from the background. Backlights help add more depth and color to your scene.


## When you're ready for more, experiment with your lighting to find your unique style!

Here are a few things you can play with:


### Soft lighting

Soft lighting comes from a large, balanced source and helps make the overall lighting in your scene look smoother. Next time you share your favorite latte recipe or film your cozy morning routine, set the scene with soft lighting to come across as warm and friendly.


### Hard lighting

Hard lighting comes from a smaller, focused source and can add drama and tension to your scene by creating defined shadows. If you're showing off your edgy outfit of the day or your vibrant cocktail recipe, consider using hard light to pair your dynamic clips with sharp highlights and bold colors.


### Play with angles

After you've mastered three-point lighting, start to play around with the angles of your lights for different effects. Maybe you move your key light higher for an overall brighter scene while sharing your recent thrift store haul. Or maybe you angle your fill light up or down to enhance the detail on the piece of furniture you just remodeled.


### Rembrandt lighting

The Rembrandt lighting technique creates a crisp shadow across your subject's face to showcase highlighted features. Place your key light 45° to the side of your subject and higher than eye level to achieve super moody and attention-grabbing results. Try this out to add more drama and intrigue while filming videos like an emotional song cover or a poetry reading.
